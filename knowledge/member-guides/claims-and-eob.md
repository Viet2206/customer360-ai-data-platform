# Claims, explanations of benefits, and medical bills

This guide explains a typical insured medical-claim flow. It does not replace a plan's explanation of benefits, provider bill, or denial notice.

## Claim lifecycle

A provider or member submits a claim containing the member, coverage, provider, diagnosis, procedure, service date, and charge information. The payer validates eligibility, coverage, coding, network status, authorization requirements, duplicate risk, and other adjudication rules. A claim may be received, pending, approved, partially approved, denied, adjusted, reversed, or paid. Status alone does not explain the financial outcome; line-level reason codes and amounts matter.

## Reading an explanation of benefits

An explanation of benefits, or EOB, is not usually a bill. It describes services processed by the health plan, the provider's charge, the allowed amount, discounts or adjustments, the plan-paid amount, and the amount assigned to the member. It may also include deductible and out-of-pocket accumulators, denial or remark codes, and appeal instructions. Compare the EOB with the provider bill and contact the plan when the records do not reconcile.

## Common denial categories

Common categories include inactive coverage on the service date, missing or invalid information, duplicate submission, excluded service, medical-necessity determination, missing prior authorization, untimely filing, coordination-of-benefits questions, or provider/network rules. A denial is not always the final outcome. Corrected claims, requested documentation, internal appeals, or external review may be available depending on the reason and plan.

## Reconciliation checklist

Confirm the member and provider, service date, procedure, billed charge, allowed amount, network status, plan payment, member responsibility, and reason codes. Check whether the provider bill reflects the payer adjustment. Do not pay an amount solely because it appears on a provider statement when the EOB shows a different responsibility; ask both parties to reconcile the difference. Preserve claim numbers, EOBs, bills, correspondence, and call reference numbers.

## Surprise-billing concerns

Federal No Surprises Act protections may apply to certain emergency services and some out-of-network services delivered at in-network facilities. The rules and exceptions depend on the coverage and facts. A deductible amount by itself is not evidence of an unlawful surprise bill. Use current CMS guidance or the No Surprises Help Desk for an actual concern.
