# Understanding health-plan cost sharing

This educational guide summarizes common US health-insurance cost concepts. Actual coverage is controlled by the member's plan documents and applicable law.

## Premium, deductible, copayment, and coinsurance

A premium is the recurring amount paid to keep coverage active, whether or not care is used. A deductible is the amount a member generally pays for covered services before the plan begins sharing those costs. A copayment is a fixed amount for a covered item or service. Coinsurance is a percentage of the allowed amount. A plan may apply different rules to medical and pharmacy benefits, and some services may be covered before the deductible.

## Allowed amount and member responsibility

The allowed amount is the maximum amount the plan recognizes for a covered service under its contract or payment rules. Member responsibility may include deductible, copayment, and coinsurance amounts. It can also include non-covered services or charges that do not count toward the out-of-pocket maximum. The billed charge, allowed amount, plan payment, and member responsibility are different fields and should not be treated as interchangeable.

## Out-of-pocket maximum

The out-of-pocket maximum is the most a member pays during a plan year for covered in-network services that count toward the limit. After the member reaches it, the plan generally pays the covered in-network costs for the remainder of that period. Premiums, non-covered services, and some out-of-network charges generally do not count. Members should verify which expenses accumulate under their specific plan.

## Before and after the deductible

Before the deductible is met, a member may pay the full allowed amount for services subject to that deductible. After it is met, copayments or coinsurance commonly apply until the out-of-pocket maximum is reached. Preventive services and other plan-design exceptions may be covered before the deductible. Family coverage may have individual and family accumulation rules.

## Example calculation

Suppose an in-network service has a $200 allowed amount and the member has met the deductible. With 20 percent coinsurance, member responsibility is $40 and the plan share is $160. If the deductible has not been met and the service is subject to it, the member may owe the full $200 allowed amount. This simplified example excludes copayments, coordination of benefits, non-covered lines, and accumulation adjustments.
