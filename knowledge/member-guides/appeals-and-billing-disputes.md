# Appeals, grievances, and billing disputes

Different problems follow different resolution paths. A claim or coverage denial, service complaint, provider bill, Marketplace eligibility decision, and surprise-billing concern should not be routed as if they were the same issue.

## Internal appeal

When a health plan denies a claim or ends coverage, an internal appeal asks the insurer to conduct a full and fair review. The denial notice should explain the reason, the information used, submission instructions, and deadlines. A strong appeal identifies the disputed decision, explains the requested outcome, and includes relevant medical records, authorization records, EOBs, bills, and plan language.

## External review

External review sends an eligible dispute to an independent third party, so the insurer does not make the final decision. Eligibility, process, and timing depend on the coverage and issue. An urgent case may qualify for an expedited pathway. Follow the denial notice and current regulator guidance rather than assuming every disagreement is eligible.

## Grievance or service complaint

A grievance commonly concerns service quality, access, communication, delay, or administrative handling rather than a request to reverse a benefit denial. Plans may use different terminology. State insurance departments, employer-plan channels, Marketplace support, or other regulators may have roles depending on the coverage source.

## Provider-bill reconciliation

For a bill, first compare it with the EOB. Confirm claim submission, allowed amount, plan payment, member responsibility, and network classification. Ask the provider to pause collection activity while a documented correction or appeal is pending when appropriate. A billing dispute should not silently change the underlying claim record; preserve an audit trail of adjustments and communications.

## Uninsured or self-pay dispute

CMS describes a federal patient-provider dispute process for certain uninsured or self-pay bills that are substantially higher than a good faith estimate. Qualification rules, dollar thresholds, time limits, fees, and documents can change. Use the current CMS eligibility tool before relying on a summary. This process is different from appealing an insured claim denial.
