# Provider networks and getting care

Network rules affect access and member cost. Always verify current participation for the specific provider, facility, service location, and plan.

## In-network and out-of-network care

An in-network provider has a contract with the health plan for the applicable product. In-network care usually has lower member cost sharing and contractual limits on payment. An out-of-network provider may have higher cost sharing, a separate deductible, no coverage except in defined situations, or potential balance-billing exposure. A provider accepting an insurer is not enough; participation can vary by plan and location.

## Verifying a provider

Use the plan's current provider directory, call the insurer using the number on the member card, and confirm with the provider office. Record the date, representative, reference number, provider identifier, facility, and plan name. Verify again before a high-cost service because directories and contracts can change. Authorization approval does not necessarily guarantee network participation or payment.

## Primary, specialist, urgent, and emergency care

Some plan types require a primary care provider or referral before specialist care. Urgent care is for conditions that need prompt attention but are not an emergency. Emergency services address symptoms a prudent person could reasonably view as an emergency. Members should seek emergency help when needed rather than delay care to perform administrative checks.

## Referrals and prior authorization

A referral commonly comes from a clinician and directs the member to another provider. Prior authorization is a payer review of coverage criteria before certain care. They are different controls and either or both may apply. Neither is a promise that every claim line will be paid, because eligibility, benefit limits, coding, medical necessity, and other conditions are evaluated when the claim is processed.

## Network issue workflow

When a directory appears inaccurate, save the directory result and contact the plan before service when practical. Ask whether an in-network alternative is reasonably available and whether continuity-of-care, gap-exception, or transition rules apply. For a processed claim, compare the EOB network indicator with prior confirmations and use the appeal instructions if the classification appears wrong.
