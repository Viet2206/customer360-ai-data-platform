# Prior authorization for medical services

Prior authorization is a process in which a payer reviews whether a planned item or service meets applicable coverage requirements before it is delivered. Requirements vary by plan, service, and date.

## What prior authorization does

The review may confirm that requested documentation supports medical necessity and other coverage criteria. A response may approve the request, deny it with a reason, request more information, or indicate that authorization is not required. An approval should identify the covered item or service, effective period or end condition, and authorization identifier when available.

## What approval does not guarantee

Authorization is not a guarantee of claim payment. The member must still be eligible on the service date, the benefit must remain covered, the provider and place of service must meet plan rules, coding must match the authorized service, and benefit limits or cost sharing may apply. Changes in treatment, provider, facility, code, quantity, or service date can require an update or new review.

## Member and provider workflow

Before scheduled care, check whether authorization is required and who must submit it. Confirm that the request includes clinical notes, diagnosis, procedure or drug details, provider, facility, requested dates, and relevant prior treatment. Track the request identifier, submission date, status, decision, approved units, and expiration. If information is missing, coordinate prompt submission rather than starting a duplicate request.

## Denial and next steps

Read the reason and requested criteria in the decision notice. A denial based on missing documentation may support a corrected submission; a coverage or medical-necessity denial may support an internal appeal. Urgent or expedited review can be available when the ordinary timeframe could seriously jeopardize health or recovery. Use the instructions and deadlines in the plan notice.

## Interoperability direction

CMS-0057-F requires certain impacted payers to improve prior-authorization processes and implement FHIR APIs on specified compliance timelines. The rule includes information about documentation requirements and responses such as approval, denial with a specific reason, or a request for more information. Exact applicability and dates vary by payer type; the current CMS rule and implementation guidance control.
